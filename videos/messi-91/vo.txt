Seville, December 2012. Lionel Messi has eighty-four goals for the year, and one more ties a record that has stood for forty years.
<break time="0.8s" />
Sixteen minutes in, he scores. That's eighty-five, level with Gerd Müller.
<break time="0.8s" />
Nine minutes later, Iniesta back-heels it into his path, and that's eighty-six. The record is his.
<break time="0.8s" />
Müller had set it back in 1972, with seventy-two goals for Bayern Munich and thirteen for West Germany.
<break time="0.8s" />
And Messi didn't stop there. He finished the year on ninety-one: seventy-nine for Barcelona, and twelve for Argentina.
<break time="0.8s" />
Guinness made it official, the most goals ever scored in a calendar year. Case closed, or so everyone thought.
<break time="0.8s" />
Then Zambia's football association spoke up, with a claim of their own: Godfrey Chitalu scored one hundred and seven goals in 1972.
<break time="0.8s" />
Forty-nine in the league for Kabwe Warriors, and fifty-eight more in cups and internationals.
<break time="0.8s" />
Researchers had rebuilt his year from the Times of Zambia, the Zambia Daily Mail and the national archives.
<break time="0.8s" />
FIFA's answer? It only keeps records for its own competitions, so it simply couldn't verify the claim.
<break time="0.8s" />
Chitalu never got the chance to argue his case. In 1993, while coaching Zambia, he died when the team's plane crashed off the coast of Gabon.
<break time="0.8s" />
And Zambia weren't alone. Flamengo said Zico had scored eighty-nine, back in 1979.
<break time="0.8s" />
Müller, eighty-five. Zico, eighty-nine. Messi, ninety-one. And Chitalu, one hundred and seven. So, who really holds the record?
