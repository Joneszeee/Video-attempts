Seville, December 2012. Lionel Messi has eighty-four goals for the year. One more ties a record that has stood for forty years.
<break time="0.8s" />
Sixteen minutes in, he scores. Eighty-five. Level with Gerd Müller.
<break time="0.8s" />
Nine minutes later, Iniesta back-heels it into his path. Eighty-six. The record is his.
<break time="0.8s" />
Müller had set it in 1972. Seventy-two goals for Bayern Munich, thirteen for West Germany.
<break time="0.8s" />
And Messi didn't stop. He finished the year on ninety-one. Seventy-nine for Barcelona, twelve for Argentina.
<break time="0.8s" />
Guinness made it official: the most goals ever scored in a calendar year. Case closed. Or so everyone thought.
<break time="0.8s" />
Then Zambia's football association spoke up. Their claim: Godfrey Chitalu scored one hundred and seven goals in 1972.
<break time="0.8s" />
Forty-nine in the league for Kabwe Warriors. Fifty-eight more in cups and internationals.
<break time="0.8s" />
Researchers rebuilt his year from the Times of Zambia, the Zambia Daily Mail and the national archives.
<break time="0.8s" />
FIFA's answer? It only keeps records for its own competitions, so it couldn't verify the claim.
<break time="0.8s" />
Chitalu never got to argue his case. In 1993, coaching Zambia, he died when the team's plane crashed off Gabon.
<break time="0.8s" />
And Zambia weren't alone. Flamengo said Zico scored eighty-nine in 1979.
<break time="0.8s" />
Müller, eighty-five. Zico, eighty-nine. Messi, ninety-one. Chitalu, one hundred and seven. So who really holds the record?
